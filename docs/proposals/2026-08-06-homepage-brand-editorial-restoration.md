# GB Law Firm Homepage — Brand Editorial Restoration Proposal

Status: user-selected visual direction; proposal only, not a replacement for `DESIGN.md`

Selected direction: **A — Brand Editorial**

## 1. Purpose

Restore the authored, premium character that was lost when the homepage stylesheet was simplified to eliminate layout breakage. The restoration must retain the stable structure while making every section feel intentionally designed, logo-aligned, and distinct.

This proposal changes presentation only. It does not add or rewrite homepage content.

## 2. Protected elements

- Do not alter the locked hero’s image, copy, layout, or typography.
- Do not alter the footer, map content, sticky mobile call bar, forms’ functional behavior, SEO, schema, analytics, or preloader behavior.
- Preserve all existing body copy verbatim.
- Preserve the current section inventory.
- Preserve the current settlement placeholder during the design-development stage, per the user’s explicit instruction.
- Treat “Where We Work” as the visual quality benchmark and preserve its overall composition.

## 3. Brand Editorial system

### Typography

- Major body-section headings use self-hosted **Cinzel 600**, matching the character of the GB logo.
- Long or operational subheadings use self-hosted **Archivo Narrow 600–700** where Cinzel would reduce readability.
- Navigation labels, numbers, buttons, indexes, and micro-labels use **Archivo Narrow 600–700**.
- Paragraphs, form labels, review text, and explanatory copy use **Source Sans 3 400–600**.
- Heading sizes must remain proportionate to their content and must not force awkward one-word wrapping.
- Body copy remains at least 16px on mobile and uses a comfortable 65–75 character measure on desktop.

### Color

- Body palette remains logo-led: deep navy, raised navy, restrained gold, warm mineral paper, cool stone, white, ink, and muted slate.
- Gold is used for authored rules, active markers, buttons, indexes, and small moments of emphasis—not as large decorative fields.
- No orange, teal, neon, gradient color system, or unrelated accent color is introduced.

### Geometry

- Sharp framed borders, inset rules, controlled asymmetry, and measured alignment replace generic rounded cards.
- Section borders must organize content rather than decorate empty space.
- Small-radius controls remain permitted where needed for usability, but large rounded containers and repeated pill-shaped section wrappers are removed.
- Decorative geometry must never overlap body copy or create false geographic/legal data.

### Rhythm

- Dense evidence and directory sections alternate with calmer editorial fields.
- Adjacent sections may not share the same silhouette, column balance, or content-entry pattern.
- Every CTA strip receives clear vertical separation and must never visually touch the section above or below.

## 4. Section treatments

### Header and awards rail

- Keep the header navy in both default and sticky states with white utility text and gold focus/active treatment.
- Increase the visual authority of the award logos through stronger contrast, a tighter rail height, balanced spacing, and a precise framed evidence strip.
- Remove the accidental empty-bottom appearance and the isolated short gold line.
- The rail must feel like verified institutional evidence, not a faded marquee.

### Firm statement

- Restore a bordered editorial composition around “Long Island Car Accident Lawyers. Trial-Ready Representation.”
- Use a top structural gold rule, a fine navy frame, deliberate asymmetry, and a compact logo-aligned Cinzel heading.
- Keep the three existing copy columns but stagger their alignment so the section does not read as a plain equal grid.

### No Fee CTA strips

- Place each strip inside its own breathing field with generous margin above and below.
- Use a sharp or minimally radiused navy plate with a gold edge marker and a gold call button.
- Prevent any strip from touching, merging with, or being clipped by neighboring sections.
- Keep the same existing content. Use placement and emphasis—not new copy—to distinguish the three appearances.

### Settlements

- Keep the `$100M+` placeholder for design development.
- Strengthen the section with a framed proof field, a vertical gold measure, and a more deliberate relationship between the number, heading, support copy, and results link.
- Avoid a plain empty navy rectangle.

### Why GB

- Replace the generic equal-card impression with a stepped editorial commitment field.
- Use borders and staggered proportions rather than floating rounded cards.
- Retain the existing icons and copy.

### Practice Areas

- Retain the louver interaction and repository photography.
- Integrate “Practice Areas” into a designed header band using Cinzel, a gold measure, and an Archivo utility label.
- Keep one dominant active panel; inactive panels remain visibly identifiable without relying on hard-to-read vertical titles alone.
- The section title must feel connected to the image system rather than floating in empty navy space.

### Types of Car Accident Cases

- Convert the plain ruled list into a premium case index.
- Use two asymmetrical indexed columns with gold route ticks, stronger row hierarchy, a vertical spine, and measured hover/focus movement.
- Keep every existing case title and paragraph.
- Avoid equal cards and avoid making the section resemble a document table.

### Reviews

- Preserve the Google score and review content.
- Strengthen the evidence hierarchy and vary card emphasis without hiding content.
- Improve review metadata size and contrast.
- Keep source attribution explicit.

### Mid-page form

- Frame the existing slogan as an editorial statement rather than a plain navy box.
- Use visible field labels, stronger form boundaries, and existing confidentiality/no-fee reassurance where already present.
- Keep the form fields and behavior unchanged.

### First 48 Hours

- Keep the seven existing steps and copy.
- Express the sequence as an authored stepped route, not a plain 2×4 list.
- Use large gold numerals, a continuous structural spine, alternating offsets, and strong reading order.
- Preserve straightforward mobile stacking.

### Trip-and-Fall / Slip-and-Fall

- Replace the plain two-column dark field with a dramatic editorial split.
- Anchor the heading inside a gold-edged frame and stagger the existing lede, explanation, and CTA along a structural vertical measure.
- Maintain generous copy measure and contrast.

### What to Expect

- Replace the plain 2×2 ruled grid with a progressive four-step composition.
- Use the existing step numbers as structural anchors and create a controlled left-to-right stair rhythm on desktop.
- Keep a single-column numbered progression on mobile.

### Where We Work

- Preserve the current composition, colors, contours, road grouping, resource action, and overall rhythm.
- Only adjust typography if required to integrate the selected Cinzel/Archivo hierarchy.

### Put Experienced Long Island Advocates on Your Side

- Replace the plain large-heading/two-column treatment with a strong editorial feature.
- Use a Cinzel title in a framed asymmetric field, stagger the two existing copy columns, and elevate the existing callout using a gold vertical rule.
- Do not repeat the Firm statement composition.

### FAQ

- Replace the pale 1990s document appearance with a contemporary navy/paper split composition.
- Use a strong Cinzel title field and a clean Archivo accordion index.
- Increase row height, active-state contrast, question size, and plus/minus clarity.
- Preserve every existing question and answer.

### Final contact, map, and footer

- Keep the final contact section dark and authoritative.
- Use visible form labels, precise framed inputs, and strong gold submission emphasis.
- Preserve the map and footer composition.

## 5. Responsive behavior

- Desktop target: 1440–1740px, with deliberate wide-screen breathing room.
- Mobile target: 375px, fully designed rather than merely stacked.
- No horizontal overflow.
- No vertical writing on mobile.
- Body copy remains at least 16px.
- Interactive controls remain at least 44px.
- CTA strips maintain clear separation on every breakpoint.
- Sticky mobile call bar must not cover the final form submit action.

## 6. Motion

- Use IntersectionObserver and CSS transitions only.
- Motion supports structural reveals: rule drawing, active-panel expansion, index emphasis, and restrained step progression.
- Do not apply the same fade-up animation to every section.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and keep all content visible without animation.

## 7. Acceptance criteria

1. Hero, copy, content inventory, form behavior, map, footer, sticky call bar, SEO, schema, analytics, and preloader behavior remain intact.
2. Awards rail is framed, legible, and free from accidental empty space.
3. Firm statement has a restored bordered editorial composition.
4. Every No Fee CTA has independent vertical breathing room.
5. Practice Areas title is integrated with the louver composition.
6. Case Types reads as a designed index, not a plain document list.
7. Trip-and-Fall, What to Expect, Advantage, and FAQ each have distinct contemporary compositions.
8. Where We Work remains visually strong and recognizable.
9. Major body headings visibly align with the logo through Cinzel while long operational headings remain readable.
10. Desktop and 375px screenshots contain no overlaps, clipping, unintended colors, or broken sections.
11. No two adjacent sections reuse the same perceived composition.
12. The implementation is verified in one complete desktop/mobile inspection, followed by at most one batched correction and one confirmation pass.

## 8. Authority note

This user-approved proposal intentionally conflicts with `DESIGN.md` v7 in two areas: the body palette and the use of Cinzel for major body headings. It does not modify `DESIGN.md`, in accordance with the repository instruction to propose design-authority changes rather than applying them silently. Implementation under this proposal therefore requires treating the user’s explicit selection of Direction A as the active override for the homepage restoration.
