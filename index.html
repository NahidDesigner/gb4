    Types of Car Accident Cases

    Our firm represents clients injured in a wide range of motor vehicle accidents across Suffolk County and Nassau County.

    • Rear-End Collisions

      These are the most common accidents on the LIE, Sunrise Highway, and Northern State Parkway. While the trailing driver is usually at fault, insurance carriers frequently try to argue brake-checking, sudden stops, or pre-existing injuries to minimize payouts.

    • Head-On Collisions

      Among the most catastrophic outcomes, and the cases where the full scope of damages is most often understated by the carrier.

    • Distracted Driving Crashes

      Phone records, telematics and scene evidence are perishable. They need to be preserved early, before they are overwritten or lost.

    • Hit-and-Run Crashes

      An unidentified driver does not end your claim. Uninsured motorist coverage frequently applies, and the claim runs against your own carrier.

    • Multi-Vehicle Pileups

      Several carriers, several defenses, and a race to assign blame elsewhere. Sorting liability early is what protects your claim.

    • Intersection and T-Bone Crashes

      Side-impact collisions where fault turns on signal timing, sight lines and witness accounts. These cases are won on evidence gathered early.

    • Drunk and Impaired Driving Crashes

      Criminal charges against the driver do not resolve your civil claim. They are separate cases, and the civil claim is where your recovery comes from.

    • Rideshare Accidents

      Uber and Lyft coverage shifts depending on whether the driver was waiting, en route, or carrying a passenger. Which policy applies changes what your claim is worth.

    • Uninsured and Underinsured Driver Cases

      When the at-fault policy cannot cover your injuries, the claim may run against your own coverage. That does not make it a friendly negotiation.

    • Pedestrian and Bicycle Crashes

      Injuries are severe, and the driver's insurer often begins by questioning where you were walking or riding.

    • Truck and Commercial Vehicle Crashes

      Federal rules, driver logs and corporate defense counsel from the first day. These files need to be built differently from the start.

    What to Do After a Crash on Long Island

    The first forty-eight hours after an accident are critical for your health and your potential legal claim. Take these steps, in this order.

    1. 01

      Call 911 and Request a Formal Police Report

      The report fixes the date, location, vehicles and any citations in an official record before memories move.

    2. 02

      Seek Medical Attention Immediately

      Adrenaline masks injury, and a gap between the crash and your first treatment is the first thing an adjuster will point to.

    3. 03

      Document the Scene Thoroughly

      Vehicle positions, damage, skid marks, signals, road conditions, and your visible injuries. More photographs than feel necessary.

    4. 04

      Gather Witness Contact Information

      Names and phone numbers. Independent witnesses are difficult to find later and hard for a carrier to dismiss.

    5. 05

      File a No-Fault Claim with Your Own Insurer

      New York law requires you to open this claim with your own insurance company to cover your initial medical bills, regardless of who caused the accident.

    6. 06

      Decline Recorded Statements from the Other Driver's Insurer

      You are not obligated to give one. These calls are taken early precisely because you do not yet know the extent of your injuries.

    7. 07

      Consult an Experienced Attorney Early

      Evidence disappears, deadlines run, and the first offer arrives sooner than most people expect.

    Trip-and-Fall and Slip-and-Fall Accidents

    Not every serious injury on Long Island happens on the road. A fall on someone else's property can leave you with broken bones, head injuries, and mounting bills, and we handle those cases too.

    Icy walkways, broken pavement, unmarked steps, wet store floors, poor lighting, and other unsafe conditions all fall under premises liability. The property owner has a duty to keep the space reasonably safe, and when they fail to do it, they can be held responsible for the harm that follows. These cases turn on proving the owner knew or should have known about the hazard, which is why photographs, incident reports, and prompt investigation matter so much. Evidence at the scene disappears quickly once the ice melts or the spill is cleaned up.

    Frequently Asked Questions About New York Accidents

    How soon should I call an attorney after a crash?

    It is best to call as soon as possible. Critical physical evidence can disappear within days, and insurance adjusters often call within 24 to 72 hours to lock you into a recorded statement before you have legal advice. Contacting us early protects your rights and prevents these early missteps.

    What happens if the at-fault driver is uninsured?

    Your own policy's uninsured motorist coverage generally steps in, and New York requires every auto policy to carry it. If the other driver carried too little insurance rather than none, underinsured motorist coverage may apply instead.

    How long do I have to file a car accident lawsuit in New York?

    Generally three years from the date of the crash for a personal injury claim. A wrongful death claim runs two years from the date of death. A claim against a city, town or public authority requires a notice of claim within 90 days.

    What do I need to prove to win a slip-and-fall or trip-and-fall case?

    That the property owner created the dangerous condition, knew about it, or should have known about it and had a reasonable opportunity to correct it. That is why photographs, incident reports and prompt investigation matter so much.

    Who pays my medical bills after a slip-and-fall accident?

    Unlike a car accident, there is no no-fault coverage for a fall. Your own health insurance usually pays first. If the claim succeeds, those costs are recovered from the responsible party, and any health insurer that paid may assert a lien against that recovery.

    Will my case actually go to trial?

    Most cases settle. But we prepare every file as though it is going to a jury, because that preparation is what makes a carrier improve its offer. If the offer never becomes fair, we are ready to try it.

    Can I still recover compensation if I am partly at fault for the accident?

    Yes. New York follows pure comparative negligence, so being partly at fault reduces your recovery by your share of the blame but does not bar it.

    New York's No-Fault Law and the Serious Injury Exception

    Your own auto insurance policy covers your initial medical bills and lost wages regardless of who caused the crash. However, to file a lawsuit against the at-fault driver for pain and suffering, your injury must meet New York's specific "serious injury" threshold. Evaluating whether your injury qualifies is one of the very first things we analyze.

    The Reality of the Insurance Carrier's First Offer

    The first number an adjuster puts in front of you usually arrives before anyone knows the full extent of your injuries, often before you have finished treating. It is an opening position, not a valuation. Accepting it generally closes the claim for good, including for treatment you have not had yet.

    Strict Legal Deadlines for Filing a Claim

    A no-fault application generally must reach your own insurer within 30 days of the accident. Most New York personal injury lawsuits must be filed within three years. Claims involving a city, town or public authority run on a far shorter clock. Missing any one of these can end an otherwise strong case.

    Social Media Posts as Defense Evidence

    A photograph at a family event or a comment about feeling better can be presented out of context as proof your injuries are not serious. Do not delete existing posts, which creates its own problems. Simply stop posting about the accident, your health and your activities.

    Trial Readiness Drives Settlement Value

    Insurance companies track which firms try cases and which reliably settle early. Complete records, retained experts and locked-down deposition testimony change the arithmetic on the other side of the table.
