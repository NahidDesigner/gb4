    Nassau County

    Denser, faster to reach, and the county where the pedestrian numbers are hardest to look at

    Car Accident Claims in Nassau County

    Nassau County packs most of Long Island's population into its western half, and it does so on a road network that was largely laid out before that population arrived. The consequence is density: more junctions, more turning movements, more pedestrians crossing roads that were designed for cars to move quickly through.

    That density changes the injury pattern. Nassau produces fewer sustained-speed highway crashes than Suffolk and considerably more of the collisions that happen at junctions, in car parks, and where somebody was on foot.

    It also changes the argument. In a dense county there are usually witnesses, usually cameras, and usually more than one version of events — which makes these cases evidence cases rather than arguments about who seemed more credible.

    Nassau crashes usually have a witness and often have a camera. Both disappear on a schedule, and neither waits for anyone to decide they want a lawyer.

    The Roads Where It Happens

    Hempstead Turnpike

    Route 24 has for years been among the most dangerous roads in the region for people on foot. Wide, fast, heavily commercial, and crossed constantly by pedestrians between bus stops and businesses.

    The Parkways

    Southern State, Northern State and the Meadowbrook. Short merges, closely spaced exits and heavy commuter volume produce merge and off-ramp collisions where fault turns on who had established the lane.

    Northern and Jericho

    Northern Boulevard and Jericho Turnpike run the county east to west through its commercial spine. Turning collisions into and out of retail frontage dominate.

    Residential Junctions

    Away from the main roads, Nassau's grid produces the ordinary intersection crash — two drivers, two accounts of the same light, and a case decided by whatever was recorded.

    Across the County

    Nassau is three towns and two cities, subdivided into a great many villages and hamlets. These are the ones we are asked about most.

    1. 01

      Hempstead

      The largest town in the county by population, and the one Hempstead Turnpike runs straight through. See our Hempstead page.

    2. 02

      Oyster Bay

      The North Shore town, running from Syosset across to the Sound. Jericho Turnpike and Route 106 carry most of it.

    3. 03

      North Hempstead

      Manhasset, Great Neck, New Hyde Park. Northern Boulevard, and the junctions where the county meets the Queens line.

    4. 04

      Long Beach

      A barrier-island city with its own department, its own traffic pattern, and a summer population several times its winter one.

    5. 05

      Glen Cove

      The other city, on the North Shore, with its own police department and its own local roads.

    6. 06

      Mineola

      Where the county's Supreme Court sits, and a dense junction of local roads around it.

    What Happens Next in Nassau

    The steps are the same as anywhere in New York. Density changes which of them are urgent.

    1. 01

      Ask About Cameras Within Days

      Nassau's commercial frontage means most junctions are overlooked by something — a shop, a petrol station, a bus. Almost all of it overwrites within days or weeks, and none of it is kept because you might want it later.

    2. 02

      Take Witness Details at the Scene

      A dense county usually means somebody saw it. That person is findable in the first hour and effectively unfindable a fortnight later.

    3. 03

      Get the Police Report

      Nassau County Police cover most of the county, but Long Beach and Glen Cove run their own departments and several villages do too. Establish who responded before requesting anything.

    4. 04

      Open the No-Fault Claim Inside 30 Days

      With your own insurer, regardless of fault. Late applications are denied on the deadline alone, whatever the merits underneath.

    5. 05

      Expect Mineola

      Civil claims in Nassau are heard in Mineola. Most matters settle without anyone appearing, but that is where it would go.

    Questions About Nassau County Claims

    I was hit crossing Hempstead Turnpike. Does being a pedestrian change the claim?

    It changes what the insurer argues rather than whether you can claim. Pedestrian cases attract an immediate suggestion that you crossed where you should not have, or stepped out, and New York's comparative fault rule means the carrier is usually arguing for a percentage rather than a denial. Where the crossings are and how far apart they are matters, and that is evidence rather than opinion.

    There is a camera on the corner. How do I get the footage?

    Quickly, and in writing. Most private systems overwrite within days or weeks and no business is obliged to keep anything nobody has asked for. A preservation request sent in the first week is frequently the difference between having the crash on video and arguing about it.

    Where would my case be heard?

    Civil claims in Nassau County are heard in Mineola. Most settle long before that, but it is where the matter would be tried.

    Long Beach police took the report, not Nassau County Police. Is that a problem?

    No. Long Beach and Glen Cove are cities with their own departments, as are several villages. It only affects where the report is requested from, which is worth getting right the first time.
